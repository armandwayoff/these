\section{Construction des maillages \ddfv} \label{sec:construction_of_the_ddfv_meshes}

Dans la \Cref{sec:hypothesis_on_the_surface_and_projection}, nous introduisons un projecteur $\fonctionens[\projvariete]{\surfpoly}{\surflisse}$ dont le but est de permettre le passage de manière univoque des objects géométriques définis sur l'approximation polyédrique $\surfpoly$ à ceux définis sur la surface lisse $\surflisse$. Ce qu'on entend par \chevron{approximation polyédrique} sera précisé dans la \Cref{sec:triangulations}. Ceci nous permettra, dans les \Cref{sec:maillages_primaux,sec:maillages_duaux,subsec:diamond_set}, de définir les maillages \ddfv; le \defini{maillage primal plat} $\ensprimalplat$, le \defini{maillage primal courbe} $\ensprimal$, le \defini{maillage dual plat} $\ensdualplat$, le \defini{maillage dual courbe} $\ensdual$, l'\defini{ensemble des diamants plats} $\ensdiamantplat$ et le \defini{maillage diamant courbe} $\ensdiamant$. Enfin, nous introduirons la régularité du maillage \ddfv dans la \Cref{sec:regularite}.

\subsection{Global coordinates} \label{sec:hypothesis_on_the_surface_and_projection}

Following \cite[Section 2.3]{dziuk_finite_2013} we introduce global coordinates in a neighbourhood of a hypersurface (see \cite[Section 2.2]{dziuk_finite_2013} for a definition of \defini{hypersurfaces}), the so-called \defini{Fermi coordinates}. This avoids working with charts and atlases when proving results and carrying out the numerical analysis. For this one introduces the \defini{oriented distance function} for $\surflisse$. Assume in the following that $\mathcal{O} \subset \R^{3}$ is bounded and open with exterior normal $\vect{n}$ and assume that $\surflisse = \partial \mathcal{O}$ is a $\Cont^k$-hypersurface ($k \geqslant 2$). The \defini{oriented distance function} for $\surflisse$ is defined by
\begin{equation} \label{eq:def_oriented_distance}
d(x) \defeq \begin{cases}
\phantom{-} \inf\limits_{y \in \surflisse} \abs{x - y} & x \in \R^{3} \setminus \overline{\mathcal{O}} \\
- \inf\limits_{y \in \surflisse} \abs{x - y} & x \in \mathcal{O}
\end{cases}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemme}[{\cite[Lemma 2.8]{dziuk_finite_2013}}] \label{lemme:fermi_coordinates}
We define
\[
U_\delta \defeq \ens[\big]{x \in \R^{3} \tq \abs{d(x)} < \delta}.
\]
Then $d \in \Cont^k(U_\delta)$, and for every point $x \in U_\delta$ there exists a unique point $\projvariete(x) \in \surflisse$ such that
\begin{equation} \label{eq:fermi_coordinates}
x = \projvariete(x) + d(x) \, \vect{n}(\projvariete(x)).
\end{equation}
$\projvariete(x)$ is the orthogonal projection of $x$ onto $\surflisse$ and $d(x)$ the oriented distance between $x$ and $\surflisse$. Moreover, we have that
\[
\grad d(x) = \vect{n}(\projvariete(x)), \quad \abs{\grad d(x)} = 1, \quad \forall x \in U_\delta. 
\]
\end{lemme}
We also extend the normal constantly in the normal direction: $\vect{n}(x) = \vect{n}(\projvariete(x))$ for $x \in U_\delta$. Thus we have introduced a global coordinate system around $\surflisse$. Every point $x \in U_\delta$ can be described by its \defini{Fermi coordinates} (normal coordinates) $d(x)$ and $\projvariete(x)$ according to \Cref{eq:fermi_coordinates}. This is illustrated by \Cref{fig:strip_u_delta}.

\subsection{Approximation of a smooth surface by a polygonal surface} \label{sec:triangulations}

Following \cite[Section 4.1]{dziuk_finite_2013}, the smooth $2$-dimensional surface $\surflisse$ ($\partial \surflisse = \varnothing$) is approximated by a surface $\surfpoly$ which lies in the strip $U_\delta$ (see \Cref{lemme:fermi_coordinates}) and which is a Lipschitz surface. We assume that the discrete triangulated surface $\surfpoly$ has the following properties. The surface $\surfpoly$ is the union of finitely many non-degenerate (closed) $2$-simplices in $\R^{3}$. We let $\ensprimalplat$ denote the set of these simplices:
\begin{equation} \label{eq:surfpoly}
\surfpoly = \bigcup_{\primalplat{K} \in \ensprimalplat} \primalplat{K}. 
\end{equation}
The vertices of the simplices are taken to sit on the smooth surface $\surflisse$. For $\primalplat{K}, \primalplat{L} \in \ensprimalplat$, either $\primalplat{K} \cap \primalplat{L} = \varnothing$ or $\primalplat{K} \cap \primalplat{L}$ is an $(3-k)$-dimensional side simplex ($k \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) common to both of the simplices $\primalplat{K}$ and $\primalplat{L}$. Note that, by \Cref{lemme:fermi_coordinates}, for every simplex $\primalplat{K} \subset \surfpoly$ there is a unique curved simplex denoted $\primal{K} \defeq \projvariete \primalplat{K} \subset \surflisse$ (see \Cref{fig:projection_3d_courbe}). In order to avoid global double covering, we assume that for each point $a \in \surflisse$ there is at most one point $x \in \surfpoly$ with $a = \projvariete(x)$. This implies that there is a bijective correspondence between the triangles on $\surfpoly$ and the induced curvilinear triangles on $\surflisse$.
