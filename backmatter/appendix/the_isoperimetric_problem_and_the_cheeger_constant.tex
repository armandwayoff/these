
\chapter{Le problème isopérimétrique et la constante de Cheeger} \label{sec:cheeger_constant}

Cette annexe a pour objectif d'introduire la notion de constante de Cheeger d'une variété compacte (une telle notion a été introduite pour les graphes dans l'\Cref{chap:graphs}) et de fournir des éléments permettant d'aboutir à la conclusion que les minimiseurs de Cheeger existent pour toute variété compacte. Le contenu de cette annexe provient de \cite[Section 3]{benson_cheeger_2016}. 

\begin{remarque}
Dans le \Cref{chap:demo_ineg_poincare}, nous utilisons la notation $\mesure$ pour désigner la mesure d'une partie de $\R^3$ (voir la \Cref{sec:triangulations}). Dans cette annexe, nous utilisons les notations $\vol_{n-1}$ (resp. $\vol_n$) pour désigner la mesure de dimension $n - 1$ (resp. $n$).
\end{remarque}

\begin{remarque}
    The following results are given in dimension $n \geqslant 1$ but will then by applied for $n = 2$ which is our setting. 
\end{remarque}

Let $\surflisse$ be an $n$-dimensional, Riemannian manifold with $\vol_n(\surflisse) < \infty$. The \textbf{Cheeger constant} of $\surflisse$ is defined to be
\[
h(\surflisse) \defeq \inf_D \frac{\vol_{n - 1}(\partial D)}{\vol_n(D)}
\]
with $D \subset \surflisse$ a smooth $n$-submanifold with boundary and $0 < \vol_n(D) \leqslant \vol_n(\surflisse) / 2$.

For a positive $n$-Hausdorff measure subset $A \subseteq \surflisse$ having $(n-1)$-Hausdorff measurable boundary $\partial A$, the \textbf{isoperimetric ratio} of $A$ is given by
\begin{equation}
    h^\ast(A) \defeq \frac{\vol_{n-1}(\partial A)}{\vol_n(A)}.
\end{equation}
The isoperimetric ratio is well-defined when $A$ is a smooth submanifold with boundary of $\surflisse$ or, more generally, when $A$ is an integral current. We also define the \textbf{isoperimetric constant} of a subset $A \subseteq \surflisse$ to be 
\begin{equation}
\overline{h}(A) = \inf_{D \subseteq A} h^\ast(D),
\end{equation}
where $D$ ranges over all subsets of $A$ which have smooth boundary. 

For $t \in \intervalleoo{0}{\vol_n(\surflisse)}$, the isoperimetric problem of volume $t$ studies the problem of finding the subset $A$ of $\surflisse$ with $\vol_n(A) = t$ and
\[
\vol_{n-1}(\partial A) = \inf \ens[\big]{\vol_{n-1}(\partial B) \tq \vol_n(B) = t}.
\]
We call such an $A$ an \textbf{isoperimetric minimizer}. For an in-depth exposition on the isoperimetric problem for Riemannian manifolds, see \cite{ros_isoperimetric_2005}.

The Cheeger constant is related to the isoperimetric problem in that we wish to minimize the isoperimetric ratio among all isoperimetric problems corresponding to volumes $t \in \intervalleof{0}{\vol_n(\surflisse)/2}$. We will call a set $D$ in $\surflisse$ with $h^\ast(D)$ well-defined and $\vol_n(D) \leqslant \vol_n(\surflisse)/2$ a \textbf{Cheeger candidate}.

As described by \cite{ros_isoperimetric_2005}, one obtains the \textbf{fundamental existence and regularity theorem} for isoperimetric minimizers of Riemannian manifolds.

\begin{theo}[Fundamental Existence and Regularity] \label{theo:fundamental_existence_and_regularity}
    When $\surflisse$ is a \textbf{smooth}, \textbf{compact}, possibly with boundary, and $t \in \intervalleoo{0}{\vol_n(\surflisse)}$, there exists a compact region $A \subset \surflisse$ such that $\partial A$ minimizes the $(n-1)$-volume among all regions of $n$-volume $t$. Further, for any such minimizer, $\partial A$ is a smooth embedded hypersurface of constant mean curvature, except for a singular set of Hausdorff dimension at most $n - 8$. Finally, if $\partial \surflisse \cap \partial A \not= \varnothing$, then $\partial \surflisse$ and $\partial A$ meet orthogonally.
\end{theo}

As suggested by \ref{theo:fundamental_existence_and_regularity} and smooth approximation techniques, it is equivalent to minimize the isoperimetric ratio over smooth submanifolds with boundary or the more general collection of rectifiable currents to find the Cheeger constant. In the same way that we have defined isoperimetric minimizers, we can define minimizers for the Cheeger constant, which we refer to as Cheeger minimizers for brevity. Specifically, a \textbf{Cheeger minimizer} is a subset $A$ of $\surflisse$ such that $\vol_n(A) \leqslant \vol_n(\surflisse)/2$ and $h^\ast(A) = h(\surflisse)$. When $\surflisse$ is compact, Buser showed that there exist volume(s) $t \in \intervalleof{0}{\vol_n(\surflisse)/2}$ so that Cheeger minimizers coincide with solutions to the isoperimetric problem of $n$-volume $t$ in $\surflisse$ \cite[Remark 3.3, Lemma 3.4]{buser_note_1982}. Therefore, the existence and regularity of a Cheeger minimizer follows from \ref{theo:fundamental_existence_and_regularity}. Specifically, Buser proved:

\begin{lemme}[Buser, {\cite[Lemma 3.4]{buser_note_1982}}] \label{lemme:buser}
Let $\surflisse$ be an $n$-dimensional, compact Riemannian manifold, possibly with boundary. Then there exists $t^\ast \in \intervalleof{0}{\vol_n(\surflisse)/2}$ and a sequence of submanifolds $A_k \subset \surflisse$ with smooth boundary such that $\vol_n(A_k) = t^\ast$ for all $k$ and
\[
h(\surflisse) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{\vol_{n-1}(\partial A_k)}{\vol_n(A_k)}.
\]
\end{lemme}
Combining \ref{theo:fundamental_existence_and_regularity} and \ref{lemme:buser}, we can immediately conclude that \textbf{Cheeger minimizers exists for all compact manifolds}.
